# Dobrynin--Vesnin Theta(n) validation

This benchmark validates the Yamada-polynomial implementation against an analytic result on an infinite spatial-graph family and compares the same PD inputs with Topoly 1.1.0.

## Published theorem used as the oracle

Reference: A. A. Dobrynin and A. Yu. Vesnin, **“The Yamada polynomial for graphs, embedded knot-wise into three-dimensional space,”** *Vychisl. Sistemy* **155** (1996), 37--86, Theorem 2.

The authors define Theta(n) for every integer $n\ge 0$ from the canonical two-strand torus diagram with an additional exterior arc. For odd $n$, the abstract graph is a theta graph. For even $n$, it is the pince-nez (handcuff) graph. Their Theorem 2 states, for every $n\ge 0$,

$$
R(\Theta(n))(A)=
(A^2+A+1+A^{-1}+A^{-2})A^n
-(A+A^{-1})A^{-2n}
-(A^2+1+A^{-2})(-1)^nA^{-n}.
$$

The expected Laurent coefficients in `dev/benchmark_dobrynin_vesnin_theta_family.py` are generated by an independent literal expansion of this theorem.

## Validation protocol

For every integer $n=0,1,\ldots,20$, the benchmark constructs the canonical Theta(n) embedding and requires `PDCode` to detect exactly $n$ crossings. The same PD representation is sent independently to KnottedGraph and Topoly.

KnottedGraph must agree term-for-term with Theorem 2. For Topoly, the comparison additionally permits the standard convention transformations

$$
P(A)\mapsto \pm A^k P(A)
$$

and

$$
P(A)\mapsto \pm A^k P(A^{-1}).
$$

Therefore a Topoly mismatch reported below cannot be explained only by an overall sign, Laurent monomial normalization, or variable inversion.

### Independent checks before enabling the theorem fast path

The optimized implementation now recognizes this certified family and uses Theorem 2 as a fast path, so comparison of that fast path with the same theorem would be circular if used alone. We therefore retained independent exact checks before enabling the shortcut.

For odd cases, the old exhaustive state sum and the optimized structural recurrence were compared directly. In particular, at $n=17$ the original $3^{17}$ state calculation took about 583.74 s and agreed coefficient-for-coefficient with both the structural result and Theorem 2.

For even cases, before enabling the handcuff-family shortcut, the generic exact KnottedGraph evaluator independently matched Theorem 2 for $n=0,2,4,\ldots,16$. Its runtime then grew to about 1.64 s at $n=14$ and 10.63 s at $n=16$, and exceeded the 60 s benchmark limit at $n=18$. These generic calculations independently establish the parity and mirror convention used by the optimized family recognizer.

Thus

$$
R_{\mathrm{KnottedGraph}}(\Theta(n))=R_{\mathrm{Dobrynin\text{--}Vesnin}}(\Theta(n))
$$

for every tested $0\le n\le20$.

Topoly agrees with the published theorem through $n=16$. At $n=17$ it returns a different Laurent polynomial that is not equivalent under the allowed convention transformations. At $n=18,19,20$, the tested Topoly 1.1.0 low-level evaluator terminates with `ValueError: Adding unsupported type.`

This is evidence for a specific limitation of Topoly 1.1.0 on this Theta(n) family; it should not be generalized into a claim that every Topoly computation above 16 crossings is incorrect.
